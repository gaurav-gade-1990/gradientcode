# Board Update on AI Spend: model answer

## 1. Situation

The board isn't really asking why one budget line is over. It's asking whether management controls AI spend, and whether margin guidance is still safe. The headline overrun answers neither, because it mixes success, slippage and waste, and it ignores the savings.

| What | Number | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| Overrun | $24.6M − $18.0M = $6.6M, 36.7% over plan | The number the audit chair has already seen |
| Gross margin hit | $6.6M / $2.4B = 27.5 bp | Larger than the 10 bp cushion, so guidance is the real question |
| Already spent | $24.6M − $6.9M of Q4 = $17.7M, 98% of plan in 9 months | Only $0.3M of plan is left, so "back to plan" is not on offer |
| Extra savings | Service agent 0.5M extra contacts × $7.00 = $3.5M; content $3.1M − $3.0M = $0.1M | The net hit is $6.6M − $3.6M = $3.0M, or 12.5 bp |
| Team outside the budget | 30 × $210k (assumed) = $6.3M | The board will ask for the all-in number |

**How:**
1. Split the $6.6M by cause. **Success:** the service agent, +$2.2M, of which 0.5M extra contacts × the planned $4.00 per contact = $2.0M is volume. **Slippage:** forecasting's pipeline rebuild, +$1.8M. **Waste:** marketing, +$2.1M, and 1,000 unplanned copilot seats × $50 × 12 = $0.6M, so $2.7M. Content came in $0.1M under.
2. Out of scope: individual vendor choices and model selection. The board decides envelopes and gates, not tools.

**Decision.** Frame the update around net impact on operating profit and guidance, with the overrun split by cause, not as one number to defend.

**Trap.** Opening with $6.6M and an apology, or with a list of AI wins. The first invites the board to cut everything; the second looks like you're dodging the question the audit chair asked.

## 2. Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | What they want | How you bring them along |
|---|---|---|
| Audit committee chair (ex-CFO) | Proof that spend is controlled and the numbers reconcile | Pre-brief four days ahead with the cause split and the reconciliation to the $24.6M; no new numbers on the day |
| CFO | Guidance held at +30 bp; no surprise from the AI line | Agree the net 3.6 bp figure and the Q4 actions with the CFO before the deck is written |
| CEO | AI seen as a strength, not a control failure | Lead with control and the decisions taken; the wins follow as evidence |
| Independent directors | A short, honest view of what works | One slide, five rows, one call per row |
| COO and customer service leader | The agent kept running through the holiday peak | Say explicitly that it is not being cut, and why |
| CMO | Marketing creative kept alive | The pause is driven by a test that can't separate the lift from zero; offer a properly sized test next year |
| Initiative owners | Credit for the value they made | Each owns a value number reported quarterly |

**The one idea to hold:** the board needs to trust the people controlling the spend more than it needs any single number, so every stakeholder gets the same reconciled figures before the meeting, and the meeting contains no surprises.

## 3. Options

The options are the framings and asks you could bring. Doing nothing to the plan is one of them.

| | A. Report only | B. Cut to plan | C. Re-base with actions | D. Ask for more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The ask | Note the $24.6M forecast | Approve cuts to reach $18M | Note $22.5M; endorse two stops; approve a gated, unit-priced budget for next year | Raise the envelope to fund all five |
| Year-end spend | $24.6M | Needs $6.6M cut from $6.9M of Q4 spend | $22.5M | $24.6M or more |
| Net hit to profit | 12.5 bp, above the 10 bp cushion | Under 0 bp, but only by stopping the agent at peak | 3.6 bp, inside the cushion | 12.5 bp or more |
| What the board learns | Spend is not controlled | Management cuts its best performer | Management separates winners from losers | Overruns get rewarded |

**How:**
1. B fails on arithmetic: only $0.3M of plan is left, and the service agent is $2.0M of the $6.9M Q4 spend. Cutting it during the holiday peak sends contacts back to the $7.00 contact center, which costs more than the agent saves.
2. A and D leave guidance exposed, since 12.5 bp exceeds the 10 bp cushion.

**Decision.** C. It is the only framing that brings the net hit under the cushion without touching the line that is making money.

**Trap.** Treating "back to plan" as the responsible answer. On these numbers it is the most expensive option, and saying so is part of the update.

## 4. Decision and Trade-offs

Recommend C. Before the meeting, pause personalized marketing for Q4 (saves $1.6M) and cut copilot seats from 6,000 to 2,500 (3,500 × $50 × 3 months = $0.525M). Year-end becomes $24.6M − $1.6M − $0.525M = $22.475M, about $22.5M.

| We give up | Why it's worth it |
|---|---|
| Marketing's possible lift in peak season | Its measured lift is $1.0M of gross profit on $5.6M of spend, and the range includes zero |
| 3,500 copilot seats | 31% weekly use means 1,860 active; 2,500 seats covers them with headroom |
| A clean "on budget" story | A true one: $4.5M over, 3.6 bp net, guidance held |
| Forecasting's original $40M promise | The evidence supports about $20M; resetting it now costs less than missing it later |

**How:**
1. **Reversibility:** both stops are two-way doors. Seats are billed monthly, and marketing can restart after a properly sized test. What is not reversible is the board's trust if a second overrun follows, which is why the ask includes gates.
2. **Revisit:** at the Q2 board meeting, or earlier if any line runs more than 10% over its quarterly budget, or the agent's repeat-contact rate rises above the human baseline.

**Decision.** Re-base at $22.5M with two stops taken before the meeting, so the board endorses decisions rather than being asked to make them.

**Trap.** Asking the board to choose what to cut. That hands them a management decision, and it reads as if you don't have a view.

## 5. Economics

Compare cost and measured value per initiative this year, then net impact against the operating plan, which is what guidance is built on.

| Initiative | FY cost | Measured value this year | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service agent | $6.2M | 1.5M × $7.00 = $10.5M avoided contact-center fees | +$4.3M |
| Content | $2.4M | $3.1M agency fees avoided | +$0.7M |
| Forecasting | $6.8M | $4.5M × 20% carrying cost (assumed) = $0.9M a year, plus $4.5M one-time cash | −$5.9M this year |
| Marketing | $5.6M (Q4 paused: $4.0M) | $800M × 0.3% × 42% = $1.0M; range −$1.0M to +$3.0M | about −$4.6M |
| Copilot | $3.6M | Not measured | −$3.6M |
| **Total** | **$24.6M** | **$15.5M** | **−$9.1M** |

**How:**
1. **Net against the plan:** $4.475M over on cost, less $3.6M of savings above plan, is $0.875M, or $0.875M / $2.4B = 3.6 bp. Before the Q4 actions it was $3.0M, or 12.5 bp.
2. **Unit economics of the agent:** $6.2M / 1.5M = $4.13 a resolved contact, against $7.00 at the contact center, so each one nets $2.87. Its overrun is the cost of 0.5M more wins.
3. **All-in:** $22.5M + $6.3M of team = $28.8M against $15.5M of measured value this year. The proven pair returns $13.6M on $8.6M.
4. **Forecasting's real prize:** if the 2-region result holds per region (assumed), 9 regions release $4.5M × 9/2 = $20.25M of inventory and save $4.05M a year. That is half of the $40M promised.
5. **Next year (proposed):** agent $7.5M as a variable line at about $4.13 per resolved contact; content $2.0M; forecasting $5.0M in two tranches ($2.0M, then $3.0M if gated); one powered marketing test $0.4M; 2,500 seats × $600 = $1.5M. Total $16.4M.

**Decision.** Show the board both numbers, the gross overrun and the net 3.6 bp, plus the all-in $28.8M. Hiding any of them will cost more trust than the number does.

**Trap.** Claiming the programme "pays for itself". It doesn't yet, all-in. Two lines do, one is an investment with a measurable gate, and two weren't working.

## 6. Org and Ownership

The overrun happened because nobody owned value per initiative, and the service agent was budgeted as a fixed sum when it is a variable cost.

| Role | Who | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Accountable for the AI portfolio | You, VP of AI | Spend against envelopes, gates, the quarterly board report |
| Value owner, per initiative | Customer service leader, merchandising, supply chain, CMO, CIO | The value number each quarter, signed off by FP&A |
| Finance partner | FP&A | Monthly reconciliation and the 10% overrun trigger |
| Oversight | Audit committee | Quarterly AI portfolio review |
| Team | 30 engineers | People on the marketing line move to the forecasting rollout |

**How:**
1. Each initiative gets a business owner who signs its value number. AI spend without an owner of value isn't approved.
2. Overruns above 10% of a quarterly line go to the CFO before money is spent, not after.

**Decision.** Move ownership of value to the business, and keep ownership of spend and gates with you.

**Trap.** Answering an overrun with a central approval committee for every AI request. That slows the winners and doesn't measure value any better.

## 7. Plan and Milestones

Nine days to the meeting, then a year of gates the board can check.

| When | Milestone | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1–3 | Q4 actions decided with CFO and CEO; marketing paused; seats cut | CFO signs the $22.475M forecast |
| Day 4–5 | Pre-brief the audit chair; deck to the CEO | No open questions from the chair |
| Day 9 | Board: the slide, the ask, Q&A | Board endorses re-base and next year's structure |
| January | Next year's budget: variable agent line, tranche 1 for forecasting | — |
| Q1 | Forecasting live in 3 more regions | Inventory down and stockouts flat or better |
| Q2 board | First quarterly report against gates; forecasting tranche 2 decision | Release $3.0M only if the Q1 gate passed |

**Decision.** Take the Q4 actions before the board meeting so the update reports decisions already made.

**Trap.** Promising the board a "full review" next quarter. That defers the decision and makes the overrun look open-ended.

## 8. Risk and Governance

The biggest risk isn't the money already spent. It's a second surprise, which would cost the CFO's credibility with investors and yours with the board.

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Agent quality drops in peak volume | Repeat-contact rate within 7 days tracked weekly; route to humans if it exceeds the human baseline |
| Agent usage keeps growing | Budget per resolved contact with a ceiling of $4.50, about 9% above today's $4.13; a breach comes back to the CFO |
| Forecasting rollout doesn't repeat the 2-region result | $3.0M tranche held until Q1 gate passes |
| Marketing test still can't separate lift from zero | Size the test before spending on it; no rollout without a result |
| Board hears new numbers on the day | Pre-brief, one reconciled data set |

**Decision.** Put the 10% overrun trigger and the forecasting tranche gate into the budget itself, so a breach comes back automatically.

**Trap.** Presenting only spend risk. The board also needs to hear the quality risk in the line you are scaling.

## 9. Metrics

Report each initiative on one value metric and one guard metric, quarterly, to the audit committee.

| Success metric | Target | Guard metric |
|---|---|---|
| Agent-resolved contacts | $7.5M / $4.13 = 1.8M next year | Repeat-contact rate no worse than human-handled contacts |
| Agent cost per resolved contact | ≤ $4.50 | Customer satisfaction on agent contacts |
| Forecasting inventory reduction | $20M across 9 regions | Stockout rate flat or better |
| Total AI spend vs envelope | Within 10% each quarter | Value owners' signed value figures, reconciled by FP&A |
| Active copilot seats | ≥ 70% weekly active (today's 1,860 users on 2,500 seats is 74%) | Survey or task-time evidence before buying more |

**Decision.** Measure the agent per outcome, not as a fixed budget, so success never again reads as an overrun.

**Trap.** Reporting usage (contacts handled, pages generated) as value. The board's question is money and risk, not activity.

## 10. Communication

**Opening line:** "We'll spend $22.5M on AI this year, $4.5M over what you approved. After the savings it produced, the net hit to operating profit is under $1M, about 4 basis points, so margin guidance holds. Two initiatives pay for themselves, one is a gated bet, and I've stopped one that wasn't working."

**The one slide:**

| Initiative | Spend vs plan | Value this year | Call |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service agent | $6.2M vs $4.0M | $10.5M saved | Scale, budget per contact |
| Content | $2.4M vs $2.5M | $3.1M saved | Keep, smaller |
| Forecasting | $6.8M vs $5.0M | $4.5M inventory so far | Gate: $3.0M tranche at Q2 |
| Marketing | $5.6M → $4.0M | Can't separate from zero | Stopped |
| Copilot | $3.6M → $3.1M | Not measured | Cut to 2,500 seats |

**Hardest question 1:** "You were 37% over. Why trust next year's number?" "Because the biggest line is now priced per result: each $4.13 of agent spend comes with a $7.00 contact we don't pay for. The rest is gated: forecasting's $3.0M is released only if three more regions show the result. Anything 10% over comes to this committee before it's spent."

**Hardest question 2:** "All-in, $28.8M bought $15.5M. Why not stop?" "Two lines return $13.6M on $8.6M and are self-funding; stopping the agent saves its $7.5M but sends about 1.8M contacts back to the contact center at $7.00, roughly $12.7M of fees next year. I've stopped the one that wasn't working. Forecasting is the bet, and you'll see its gate in Q2."

| Audience | One line |
|---|---|
| Board | The opening line above |
| Audit chair (pre-brief) | "Here is the $24.6M reconciled, the split by cause, and what we cut." |
| CFO | "3.6 bp net, inside your cushion; next year's agent line moves with volume." |
| CMO | "Paused, not killed: one test of a size that can give an answer." |

**Decision.** Lead with the net number and the actions, then the slide; the overrun is mentioned in the first sentence, not hidden. Report quarterly to the audit committee from now on.

**Trap.** Leading with the wins. The audit chair asked a control question, and answering a different one sounds like spin.

# AI Hiring Plan and Leveling: model answer

## 1. Situation

The question isn't "how do we hire 12 AI engineers". It's how to get a team in seats before the month-6 back-to-school release, inside a $3.6M run-rate, under a pay rule the rest of engineering will accept as fair. The VP Product's mix fails the budget at market and fails the calendar at band.

| What | Number | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| VP Product's mix at band | 2 × $370k + 6 × $290k + 4 × $230k = $3.40M | Fits the budget on paper |
| Same mix at market | 2 × $460k + 6 × $350k + 4 × $260k = $4.06M | $460k over a cap that won't move |
| Acceptance at band | 3 of 11 = 27%; 5 of 8 declines cited a higher level | Band offers fill too slowly, and leveling is under pressure |
| Revenue clock | $6M / 12 = $500k of first-year ARR per month late | The calendar has a price |
| Internal equity | 40 L5 engineers at band; AI L5 at market is $60k higher | Any premium needs a job-based reason open to everyone |

**How:**
1. Treat cost and level mix as one decision. The cap is fixed, so the mix is the lever.
2. Out of scope: the product designs themselves, and pay for engineering outside the AI role.
3. The market figures are a survey, not offers; the 70% acceptance at market is an assumption to watch.

**Decision.** Frame it for the exec staff as "a team in seats by month 5 at $3.6M, with one pay rule", not as a request for 12 hires.

**Trap.** Taking the VP Product's level mix as given. A brand-new team doesn't need two staff engineers on day one, and the mix is where the money is.

## 2. Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | What they want | How you bring them along |
|---|---|---|
| CFO | $3.6M held, and no surprise in year two | The mix priced at market, with $90k of headroom and no year-two surprise |
| HR and the comp committee | One fair, defensible pay rule | An AI range tied to written role criteria, open to anyone who passes the loop |
| VP Product | 12 people and two staff engineers for two products | The tutor team is fully staffed; triage starts with 3 and lands 2 months later; the 12th head is revisited at month 6 |
| Recruiting | Offers candidates accept | Market-rate ranges and a calibrated level before any comp talk |
| Engineering managers losing transfers | Their roadmaps intact | Backfills from engineering's plan; transfers start after a 30-day handover |
| The 40 L5 engineers outside AI | Not to be paid less than strangers for the same level | A published path into the AI role through the same loop |
| The 4 transfers | A real move, not a trial | Same loop passed, so the AI range applies from their start date |

**The one idea to hold:** the pay rule has to be one everyone can see, reach and defend, because the people judging it are the 40 engineers who stay where they are.

## 3. Options

Five options, scored on the cap, the calendar and fairness. Every option uses the 4 transfers (2 L5, 2 L4).

| | A. Bands, VP's mix | B. Premium for new hires only | C. AI range, VP's mix | D. AI range, 1 / 5 / 5 | E. AI range, 1 / 3 / 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heads (L6 / L5 / L4) | 12 (2 / 6 / 4) | 12 | 12 (2 / 6 / 4) | 11 (1 / 5 / 5) | 12 (1 / 3 / 8) |
| Run-rate | $3.40M | Not allowed | $4.06M | $3.51M | $3.59M |
| External hires | 8 | — | 8 | 7 | 8 |
| Offers needed | 8 / 27% = 29 | — | 8 / 70% = 11 | 7 / 70% = 10 | 11 |
| Last start (month) | 1.5 + 29 / 4 + 1 ≈ 10 | — | ≈ 5.4 | 1.5 + 10 / 4 + 1 = 5 | ≈ 5.4 |
| Fairness | Fair, but uncompetitive | Fails the comp committee | Fair | Fair | Fair |

**How:**
1. **B is off the table.** The comp committee won't approve it, and it would pay strangers more than the 40 L5 engineers.
2. **A is cheapest and latest.** At 27% acceptance and 4 offers a month, the team is complete around month 10.
3. **E fits but is thin.** One staff and 3 seniors would lead 8 mid-level engineers across two products and shared evaluation work.

**Decision.** Shortlist D, and keep E as the fallback if senior offers stall.

**Trap.** Solving the budget by quietly leveling people lower than the loop says. That is the same inequity as B, hidden inside the ladder.

## 4. Decision and Trade-offs

Recommend D: an AI market range for the AI role at every level, and 11 hires at 1 L6, 5 L5 and 5 L4, for $3.51M. That includes the 4 transfers, and 7 external hires: 1 L6, 3 L5 and 3 L4.

| We give up | Why it's worth it |
|---|---|
| The 12th head | It buys a mix of 6 senior-or-above to 5 mid-level, which can run two products |
| The second staff engineer | One staff engineer sets the architecture; a second staff role is earned later |
| Triage on 3 engineers, not 4 | 24 / 3 = 8 months instead of 6; it has no revenue, the tutor release does |
| The pay gap with non-AI L5s | It's tied to a role with written criteria and open to anyone through the loop |

**How:**
1. **Reversibility.** The level mix is reversible: the 12th seat can still be filled. The AI range is close to one-way, since cutting pay is not realistic, so it gets a yearly review against the survey instead.
2. **Revisit at month 6**, and ask the CFO for $260k − $90k of unused headroom = $170k to fill the 12th seat as an L4 if:
   - the tutor is live in the back-to-school release with at least 1 paying pilot district;
   - triage is more than 2 months behind its plan;
   - market acceptance is at or above 60%.

**Decision.** D, because it is the only option that is fair, fits the cap and fills the team before the release.

**Trap.** Promising the VP Product all 12. Saying plainly what is given up, and when you'll revisit it, is what earns the trust to ask for the 12th seat later.

## 5. Economics

Three-year run-rate from the level mix, plus the price of a late team. All compensation figures are the assumed band and survey figures from the brief.

| | A. Bands | C. AI range, VP's mix | D. AI range, 1 / 5 / 5 | E. AI range, 1 / 3 / 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Run-rate | $3.40M | $4.06M | 460 + 5 × 350 + 5 × 260 = $3.51M | 460 + 3 × 350 + 8 × 260 = $3.59M |
| 3-year run cost | $10.20M | $12.18M | $10.53M | $10.77M |
| Late-team cost | Team ≈ month 9.8, 3.8 months late × $500k = $1.9M deferred ARR (assumes the release waits for the team) | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Against the cap | Under | $460k over | $90k under | $10k under |

**How:**
1. **Why the range is scoped to the role.** Extending it to all 40 L5 engineers would cost 40 × ($350k − $290k) = $2.4M a year.
2. **Transfers.** Their raises (2 × $60k + 2 × $30k = $180k a year) are inside D's $3.51M. Their backfills are paid from engineering's own plan, so the company's total cost is higher than the AI budget shows.
3. **Opportunity cost.** The 12th head and the 2 months of triage; triage cuts support tickets rather than earning ARR.

**Decision.** D costs $330k more than A over 3 years and avoids about $1.9M of deferred ARR, and it leaves $90k of headroom: a sign-on fallback until month 6, then part of the 12th seat.

**Trap.** Calling band offers "cheaper". At 27% acceptance the cheapest plan is the one that costs four months of the school year.

## 6. Org and Ownership

Separate the people who decide the level from the people who negotiate the offer.

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| You | The plan, the level mix, the AI role criteria and the month-6 revisit |
| Leveling panel (2 AI leads and 1 engineering lead outside AI) | The level, decided from the loop before any pay talk |
| Recruiter | Pipeline and offers within the range for the decided level |
| HR (comp) | The AI range, its written criteria and its yearly review |
| Engineering managers | Transfer handovers and their own backfills |

| Workstream | People |
|---|---|
| AI tutor | 5, led by an L5 |
| Shared evaluation and platform | 3, led by the L6 |
| Help-desk triage | 3, led by an L5 |

**Decision.** The level is fixed before comp is discussed, and the recruiter can move within the range but never across a level.

**Trap.** Letting the hiring manager both level and close. Under deadline pressure, levels drift up to win offers.

## 7. Plan and Milestones

Transfers fill the first seats; external hires follow, staff engineer first.

| When | Milestone | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Week 2 | Exec staff approve plan; comp committee approves the AI range and criteria | — |
| Month 1 | 4 transfers start; staff engineer search opens first | — |
| Months 1.5–4 | 10 offers at 4 a month for 7 external hires | **Gate:** acceptance ≥ 60% after the first 5 offers, or switch to fallback |
| Month 5 | Last external hire starts | 11 of 11 in seat |
| Month 6 | Back-to-school release; revisit the 12th head | Decision record to the CFO |
| Month 12 | First yearly review of the AI range against the survey | — |

**Decision.** Start the staff engineer search first, because the hardest seat takes longest and sets the architecture.

**Trap.** Opening all 7 roles at once with one recruiter. Offers queue up and the staff search starves.

## 8. Risk and Governance

The process is the fairness. Write it down before the first offer.

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| The range looks like favouritism | Written AI role criteria; the range applies by role and level, to transfers and external hires alike |
| Level inflation to close offers | Panel levels before comp; flexibility only within the range or by start date |
| Inconsistent internal and external bar | Same structured loop and scoring for both; pass rates compared each quarter |
| Offers still declined at market | Fallback to E's mix, or a sign-on bonus within the $90k headroom |
| Non-AI engineers feel shut out | The transfer track stays open with a published schedule |
| Bias in the loop | Structured questions, scored rubrics, diverse panels, HR audit of outcomes |

**Decision.** HR owns the written criteria and audits the outcomes, so the range has a documented, job-based reason if anyone challenges it.

**Trap.** Treating "market rate" as its own justification. Without written role criteria, a pay gap between two L5s is hard to defend.

## 9. Metrics

Each success metric is paired with a guard.

| Success metric | Target | Guard metric |
|---|---|---|
| Offer acceptance | ≥ 70% | Share of hires leveled above the panel's call: zero |
| Seats filled | 11 by month 5 | First-year regretted attrition of new hires |
| Tutor release date | Month 6 | Quality bar at release, set with product |
| Run-rate | ≤ $3.6M | Sign-on spend kept inside the $90k headroom |
| Transfer track use | Open every quarter | Attrition among L5 engineers outside AI, against last year |

**Decision.** Report non-AI L5 attrition next to the AI hiring numbers, because that is where the cost of getting fairness wrong shows up.

**Trap.** Measuring only time to fill. A fast fill that inflates levels or drives out the 40 is a slower failure.

## 10. Communication

One plan, cut for each audience.

| Audience | One line |
|---|---|
| Exec staff | "11 in seats by month 5 for $3.51M, back-to-school release at month 6, and the 12th head revisited then." |
| CFO | "$90k under the cap; the 12th seat is a $170k ask at month 6, only on results." |
| VP Product | "The tutor is fully staffed; triage starts with 3 and lands 2 months later; one staff engineer, not two." |
| Comp committee | "One range for the AI role at each level, transfers included, with written criteria." |
| Engineering org | "The AI range comes with the role; here is the loop and the next transfer window." |
| Recruiting | "Level first, then offer within the range; never across a level." |

**How:**
1. Tell the engineering org about the range and the transfer track before external offers go out.
2. Weekly hiring status to the VP Product; monthly to exec staff until month 6.

**Decision.** Announce the path into the range at the same time as the range itself, so it reads as an opportunity, not a gap.

**Trap.** Keeping the range quiet. Pay comes out within weeks, and then the story is told by whoever found it.

### Context

You are the VP of AI at a publicly listed home-furnishings retailer: $2.4B annual revenue, 160 stores and a large online business. Last December the board approved an $18M AI budget for this fiscal year across five initiatives. Q3 has just closed, and the year-end forecast is $24.6M. The audit committee chair, a former CFO, has asked why. The CEO has given you a 10-minute slot, plus questions, at the board meeting in 9 days, and wants your recommendation on what to tell the board and what to ask it for.

### What you have

- **The budget.** The $18M covers model and API usage, vendors, cloud, software seats and contractors. It does not include the AI team of 30, who sit in the technology budget (fully loaded cost assumed at $210k a person).
- **The five initiatives**, in $M:

| Initiative | FY plan | FY forecast | Q4 forecast (Oct–Dec) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer service agent | 4.0 | 6.2 | 2.0 |
| Product content generation | 2.5 | 2.4 | 0.5 |
| Demand forecasting | 5.0 | 6.8 | 1.9 |
| Personalized marketing creative | 3.5 | 5.6 | 1.6 |
| Employee copilot seats | 3.0 | 3.6 | 0.9 |

- **Customer service agent.** Forecast to resolve 1.5M contacts this year against 1.0M in the plan. "Resolved" means no repeat contact within 7 days. Contacts it does not resolve go to an outsourced contact center that bills $7.00 per contact. Its spend is almost all usage, so it rises with volume. Q4 is the holiday peak.
- **Product content generation.** Rewrote 180,000 product pages and replaced an agency whose fees would have been $3.1M this year. The plan assumed $3.0M.
- **Demand forecasting.** Live in 2 of 9 regions. Those 2 regions cut inventory by $4.5M with 6 percent fewer stockouts. The business case promised $40M of inventory reduction across all 9. Inventory carrying cost is assumed at 20 percent a year. The overrun came from rebuilding the data pipeline.
- **Personalized marketing creative.** Generates email and ad variants. A holdout test measured +0.3 percent incremental revenue, with a 95 percent range of ±0.6 points, on $800M of digital revenue at a 42 percent gross margin.
- **Employee copilot seats.** 6,000 seats at $50 a month, billed monthly. 31 percent are active weekly. The plan had 5,000 seats.
- **The plan's savings.** The operating plan counted only two AI savings this year: $7.0M from the service agent (1.0M contacts × $7.00) and $3.0M of agency fees from content. Nothing was assumed for the other three.

### The constraint

The CFO has guided investors to 30 basis points of operating-margin improvement this year, and the plan holds only 10 basis points of cushion. By the end of Q3, $17.7M of the $18M was already spent, so the year cannot come back to plan without switching off the service agent during the holiday peak. Its spend is the largest part of Q4.

### What a strong answer covers

- How the overrun splits between success, slippage and waste, and what it does to margin guidance once savings are counted.
- What can still change in Q4, and what can't.
- A portfolio view that is honest about the uneven ROI, including the team cost.
- What you ask the board for, and how next year's budget avoids the same surprise.
- The one slide, the opening line, and your answers to the two hardest board questions.

### Context

You are the head of AI at a digital freight brokerage: $900M a year of gross bookings at a 14 percent net revenue margin, 45,000 truckloads a month, and 220 human brokers who quote shippers, find carriers and book loads. Yesterday the CEO told the exec staff that at the Shipper Summit, the company's customer conference in 8 weeks, she will announce an AI agent that "books loads autonomously for all shippers", live that day. She has already told two anchor shippers it is coming. Your team built the prototype, and you don't believe that scope is safe in 8 weeks. You have a one-to-one with her on Friday.

### What you have

- **The prototype.** On a backtest of 3,000 loads across 12 lanes, the agent's quoted rate landed within 5 percent of the broker's final rate on 86 percent of loads. 9 percent were under-quoted, by 8 percent on average, which gives margin away. 5 percent were over-quoted, which tends to lose the load.
- **Broker time.** Quoting, finding a carrier, vetting it and booking takes a broker about 25 minutes a load. With the agent drafting the quote and a carrier shortlist and a broker approving, it took 6 minutes in the prototype.
- **Fraud.** Brokers manually vet every carrier. They reject 0.4 percent of carrier offers as suspected fraud: fake carriers who take a load and steal the cargo, or re-broker it and disappear. Even so, 38 fraudulent carriers got through last year, costing $2.09M in cargo claims.
- **Automated vetting.** A carrier-vetting provider can check authority, insurance and identity signals on every booking, for $18k a month. Their onboarding takes 10 weeks from contract signature. Procurement is waiting on your go-ahead.
- **Team and estimates.** 7 engineers and 1 PM, fully loaded cost assumed at $230k a year each. The team estimates:
  - the broker co-pilot (agent drafts, broker approves) at 6 weeks;
  - full autonomous booking for all shippers at 20 weeks, which includes 3 weeks to integrate the vetting provider after onboarding and a 4-week shadow run before go-live.
- **Money.** The CEO has offered up to $400k for contractors to hit the date.
- **The anchors.** The two anchor shippers book $60M a year between them. Both are evaluating a competitor that says it books autonomously, and both contracts renew in 18 weeks.
- **Trusted carriers.** 1,400 carriers have hauled at least 25 loads for us with no claims in two years. They carry 62 percent of the anchors' loads.

### The constraint

The summit date is fixed and public, and the CEO has made a promise to the anchors. Autonomous booking without automated vetting removes the human check that stops cargo theft, and the vetting provider's 10-week onboarding can't be bought down. It doesn't start until the contract is signed.

### What a strong answer covers

- What "all shippers, autonomous, in 8 weeks" would cost in margin and fraud, as a number.
- What the $400k can and can't buy.
- A counter-offer that gives the CEO a true story on stage and keeps the anchors' promise.
- How you push back without looking like the person who said no.
- The opening line, the one paragraph you leave with her, and your answers to her two hardest questions.

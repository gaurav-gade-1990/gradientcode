### Context

You are the Director of Machine Learning at an email security company: $260M ARR, 6,500 business customers. Your team of 11 ML engineers trains and ships the phishing detection models. On Monday your principal engineer, Maya, told you she has an offer from a large tech company and needs to answer within 10 days. Maya built the training pipeline six years ago and is the only person who has run the quarterly model refresh end to end. The next refresh is due in 10 weeks.

### What you have

- **The offer.** Maya's current total compensation is $360k a year; the offer is $520k (both assumed, as she described them). She said the money matters, but the main reason is 18 months spent mostly on maintenance and on-call. She wants to lead the new detection work for business email compromise (BEC).
- **The BEC initiative.** Funded, with a launch planned in 9 months. Sales forecasts $3M of new ARR in its first year. The lead role is open and has been searched externally for 3 months with no finalist. It is budgeted at $400k a year total compensation (assumed); a senior ML engineer costs about $300k (assumed).
- **Comp policy** (assumed). The principal band is $300k–$400k total compensation. You and HR can approve an off-cycle increase up to the band maximum. Anything above it needs the comp committee, which next meets in 6 weeks. You can grant one-time retention equity of up to $150k, vesting over 2 years, without the committee. The company's two other principal engineers, on other teams, are at $370k and $385k.
- **The refresh.** It retrains the models on the latest phishing samples. The last time it slipped by a quarter, two years ago, customer-reported misses rose 2.1× and 2 customers worth $1.1M of ARR left, citing missed attacks. Ravi, a senior engineer, has shadowed one refresh. The runbook covers about half the steps.
- **On-call.** A rotation of 4, including Maya.
- **If Maya leaves.** Principal ML searches take about 6 months here (assumed, given the BEC search). An agency fee is about 25% of first-year cash, assumed at $70k. A new principal is assumed to take 6 months to reach full speed.

### The constraint

Maya has to answer in 10 days, and the comp committee doesn't meet for 6 weeks, so you can't promise any cash above the $400k band maximum. And the thing she wants most, leading BEC, takes her off the refresh 10 weeks before it is due, when she is the only person who has run it.

### What a strong answer covers

- What Maya actually wants, and how you find out before you make an offer.
- What you can offer within your authority in 10 days, and what you shouldn't promise.
- What losing her would cost, and what keeping her costs, over the same horizon.
- How the refresh gets done whichever way she decides.
- How you stay fair to the other principals and to the team, who will notice.

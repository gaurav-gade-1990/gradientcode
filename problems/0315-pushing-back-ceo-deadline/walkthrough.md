## 1. What the problem is really asking

On the surface this is a schedule dispute: the CEO says 8 weeks, the team says 20. Underneath, it's a test of how you manage up. The interviewer wants to see whether you can turn "no" into a better "yes": protect the company from a risk the CEO hasn't priced, while giving her what she actually needs.

Three facts in the brief carry the case:

1. **The CEO's real goal is the anchors, not the feature.** She told two shippers, worth $8.4M of net revenue, and both renew in 18 weeks. "All shippers, autonomous" is her guess at how to keep them. Once you see that, week 18 matters as much as week 8.
2. **The critical path is outside your team.** The vetting provider takes 10 weeks from signature, then 3 weeks to integrate and a 4-week shadow run. That's 17 weeks, and no amount of contractor money shortens it. The $400k is real, but it buys a different thing than she thinks.
3. **The risk isn't the model, it's the carrier.** Pricing errors cost margin, but the big number is cargo theft once a human stops vetting carriers. Autonomy is safe today only with carriers you already trust.

A strong answer makes a counter-offer, prices each option in dollars a month, says exactly what the money buys, and writes the words the CEO will say on stage.

## 2. The numbers that drive the decision

Work these out first. Every later section reuses them.

**Unit economics of a load.**
- Monthly bookings: $900M / 12 = $75M; per load $75M / 45,000 = **$1,667**.
- Margin per load: $1,667 × 14% = **$233**. Monthly margin: 45,000 × $233 = **$10.5M**.

**Pricing exposure if the agent books everything.**
- Under-quoted loads: 45,000 × 9% = **4,050 a month**, each $1,667 × 8% = **$133** low.
- 4,050 × $133 = **$540k a month**, about 5% of monthly margin.
- Over-quoted loads: 45,000 × 5% = 2,250 × $233 = up to **$525k a month** of margin at risk if they're lost.

**Fraud exposure without vetting.**
- Suspect offers: 45,000 × 0.4% = **180 a month**.
- Average claim: $2.09M / 38 = **$55k**.
- Assume 1 in 10 suspect offers becomes a loss once nobody vets (labelled assumption): 18 × $55k = **$990k a month**.
- Total for option A: $540k + $990k = **$1.53M a month**.

**The calendar.**
- Critical path: 10 + 3 + 4 = **17 weeks** from signature.
- Option A runs unvetted from week 8 to week 17: 9 weeks / 4.33 weeks a month × $1.53M = **about $3.2M**.
- The team's 20 weeks includes 3 weeks of work off the critical path. The $400k moves 20 to 17 if contractors can take that work (assumed). That's **3 weeks** for $400k, and it moves full autonomy from after the renewal (week 18) to before it.

**The anchors.**
- Net revenue: $60M × 14% = **$8.4M**, which is 8.4 / 126 = **6.7%** of the company's.
- Loads: $60M / $1,667 / 12 = **3,000 a month**. On trusted carriers: 3,000 × 62% = **1,860 a month**.
- Under-quote ceiling on those: 1,860 × 9% × $133 = **$22.3k a month**, before the price band removes the worst of them.

**The co-pilot.** 45,000 × (25 − 6) minutes = **14,250 broker hours a month**, 76% of the 45,000 × 25 / 60 = 18,750 hours brokers spend on loads.

## 3. How to spend your time

Forty-five minutes, about ten per pillar.

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Read the brief. Write down $1,667 and $233, $540k, $990k, 17 weeks, 1,860 loads, week 18. Circle "from contract signature" and "already told two anchor shippers". |
| 5–13 | **Pillar A.** Restate her goal; build the stakeholder table with the one-to-one in it. |
| 13–27 | **Pillar B.** Four answers on one table, the counter-offer, what the $400k buys, and the exposure comparison. |
| 27–36 | **Pillar C.** Owners for the two risk levers, the plan with its week-6 gate, the theft risk. |
| 36–43 | **Pillar D.** Metrics with guards; the opening line, the paragraph and the two answers, out loud. |
| 43–45 | Check every number is derived or labelled. |

## 4. Pillar A: Frame

### Situation

**The question it answers:** what is the CEO really asking for, and which numbers frame the conversation?

**How to reason.** Rewrite her request as her goal: the anchors renew, and the summit shows something real. Then frame it with numbers she'll recognise: margin per load, what autonomy everywhere costs a month, the 17-week path, and the two dates. Week 8 is the stage; week 18 is the money.

Say what's out of scope. The summit date is public and isn't moving, so don't spend the one-to-one asking for it.

**The trap.** Leading with your estimate. "20 weeks" sounds like a no, and it puts your plan against hers.

**Rubric criteria earned:** her goal restated; the $540k and $990k derived with the fraud rate labelled; the 17-week path; week 18 named.

### Stakeholders

**The question it answers:** who has a stake in the deadline, and how do you bring each along?

**How to reason.** Start with where you push back. A CEO corrected in front of her staff has to defend her position; one given a better option in private can adopt it. Then hand each risk to the person who already owns it: compliance owns carriers, the COO's team owns pricing. That turns your pushback into their expertise. The CRO needs something to sell and a list of what not to claim. The anchors want something live. The brokers want the agent to make their work faster, not to create clean-up.

Close with one idea: everyone wants the anchors to renew; they differ only on how much risk to take, so price each risk for the person who cares about it.

**The trap.** Bringing the CTO or the board in first, around the CEO. It wins the argument and loses the relationship.

**Rubric criteria earned:** the one-to-one; compliance and the COO given roles; anchors and brokers covered; one idea to hold.

## 5. Pillar B: Decide

### Options

**The question it answers:** what answers could you give on Friday, and how does each fare?

**How to reason.** Put four answers on one table: yes as asked, a flat no, yes to the date with a different scope, and a vision-only announcement. Score them on what's true on stage, monthly exposure, when all-shipper autonomy arrives, and whether her promise to the anchors survives.

A carries about $3.2M of exposure from week 8 to week 17. B breaks her promise and lands autonomy after the renewal. D is a slide with no product, which an anchor evaluating a competitor will see through. C keeps the date and the promise, and its exposure is capped at $22.3k a month before the price band.

**The trap.** Framing it as yes or no. The counter-offer is the pushback.

**Rubric criteria earned:** the three main answers on one table; A's $3.2M; the 1,860 loads and their ceiling; scored on stage truth and the promise.

### Decision and Trade-offs

**The question it answers:** what do you recommend, and what does it give up?

**How to reason.** Recommend C, and be exact about the $400k. The vetting path is 17 weeks whatever you spend, so the money doesn't buy week 8. It buys 3 weeks: contractors take the parallel work so the team's 20 weeks fits inside the 17-week path. That moves full autonomy from week 20 to week 17, before the renewal. Say this plainly; it's what makes the rest believable.

Then the one action that matters most this week: sign the vetting contract. The clock starts at signature, so every week of delay pushes week 17.

Give-ups: "all shippers" on stage, the $400k, 38% of anchor loads (carriers outside the trusted list), and her exact wording. Reversibility: a feature flag per shipper. An announcement followed by a theft isn't reversible.

**The trap.** Accepting the money as if it bought the date. If you take $400k and still miss "all shippers", you've lost her trust twice.

**Rubric criteria earned:** one recommendation that keeps the date; what the $400k buys; sign this week; a measurable trigger (the week-6 gate).

### Economics

**The question it answers:** what money and risk sit behind each answer?

**How to reason.** Compare exposure over the same window, week 8 to week 17. A: about $3.2M. B: zero, but the anchors are at risk. C: at most 9 / 4.33 × $22.3k = $46k, plus $400k of contractors and $18k a month for vetting, which every option needs anyway. Separate new spend from the team: 7 × $230k (assumed) = $1.61M a year, on payroll whatever she decides.

Value the co-pilot honestly: 14,250 broker hours a month is capacity to grow without hiring, not cash this quarter. And name the opportunity cost: the team builds two products at once, so nothing else ships for 17 weeks.

**The trap.** Comparing $400k with the $8.4M anchors and calling it obvious. The comparison that matters is $3.2M of exposure for the same anchors.

**Rubric criteria earned:** exposure over one window; new spend separated from payroll; co-pilot hours not claimed as cash; opportunity cost.

## 6. Pillar C: Execute

### Org and Ownership

**The question it answers:** who owns the risky decisions?

**How to reason.** You own the counter-offer and the dates. Give the two risk levers to the people who own those risks: compliance controls the trusted-carrier list, and the COO's pricing lead sets the lane bands the agent books inside. Stage words are agreed by the CEO, the CRO and you, in writing.

**The trap.** Keeping every decision in the AI team. It makes the agent look like an experiment rather than a brokerage product.

**Rubric criteria earned:** one owner; list and band with compliance and pricing; stage words agreed jointly.

### Plan and Milestones

**The question it answers:** what happens between Friday and week 18?

**How to reason.** Two anchor dates. Week 0: one-to-one, vetting signed, contractors started. Weeks 1–6: co-pilot for everyone and anchor autonomy behind a flag. Weeks 6–8: shadow mode on anchor lanes, where the agent decides and a broker books, with a gate: at least 90% of in-band bookings within 5% of the broker's rate, and zero non-trusted carriers chosen. Week 8: the summit, with anchors live if the gate passed, co-pilot only if not. Weeks 10–17: vetting live, integration, shadow on all lanes, then autonomy for everyone.

**The trap.** No gate before the summit. You'd find out on stage.

**Rubric criteria earned:** a measurable pre-summit gate; vetting by week 10 and autonomy by week 17; the co-pilot-only fallback.

### Risk and Governance

**The question it answers:** what could go wrong after the announcement, and what controls it?

**How to reason.** The worst outcome is a theft on an autonomously booked load in the first month. Trusted carriers lower that risk but don't remove it: fraudsters take over real carriers' accounts and change where payment goes. So any change to a carrier's bank details removes it from the list until a broker re-checks it. Add the price band, the fallback if the gate fails, a one-for-one slip rule if vetting is late, and a written list of what sales may claim.

**The trap.** Trusting the 86% backtest. At full scale, 9% under-quoted is 4,050 loads a month.

**Rubric criteria earned:** theft named; bank-detail removal; controls in the booking path.

## 7. Pillar D: Land

### Metrics

**The question it answers:** how do you know the counter-offer worked?

**How to reason.** Pair each success metric with a guard. Autonomous anchor loads (target 1,860 a month) against margin per autonomous load versus broker-booked. Broker minutes per load (6 with the co-pilot) against the over-quote rate. Week 17 against zero fraud incidents. Anchor renewals against anchor complaints about wrong bookings.

**The trap.** Counting bookings alone. Under-quoting makes volume rise fastest.

**Rubric criteria earned:** paired guards; margin per autonomous load; renewals tracked.

### Communication

**The question it answers:** what exactly do you say to the CEO?

**How to reason.** Lead with what you can deliver, then the risk, then the alternative: "I can get you a true story on stage in 8 weeks with both anchors live on it. What I can't do is 'all shippers' by then without about $1.5M a month of fraud and pricing risk, so here's what I'd announce instead."

Then leave her one paragraph she could read on stage. It names the anchors' live autonomy, the co-pilot for everyone, week 17, and what her $400k bought. Writing her words for her is the most persuasive thing you can do.

Rehearse the two hardest questions.

- **"Why can't we match the competitor?"** Give a date, week 17, and the priced risk of matching them sooner.
- **"Are you making me walk it back?"** No: the two customers she told get what she promised.

Then commit: back her decision in public, and send a weekly update until week 17.

**The trap.** Pushing back in the staff meeting, or bringing only a risk list. Either way she has to choose between you and her promise.

**Rubric criteria earned:** an opening that leads with delivery; the stage paragraph; both hard answers.

## 8. The decisions that separate strong answers

**Counter-offer versus refusal.** Weak answers argue 20 weeks against 8. Strong answers find the slice that's true at week 8, autonomous for the two anchors on trusted carriers, and offer it with stage words.

**Pricing what money buys.** Seeing that the critical path is external, and that $400k buys 3 weeks, not 12, shows you understand delivery at director level. It also moves the date before the renewal, which is the point.

**Giving away the risk levers.** Handing the trusted list to compliance and the price band to pricing turns a fight about AI readiness into a decision made by the people who own the risks.

## 9. Common mistakes

- **Saying "20 weeks" first.** It frames you as the obstacle.
- **Forgetting the anchors renew in 18 weeks.** That makes week 17 worth $400k.
- **Ignoring fraud.** Pricing errors are the visible risk; cargo theft is the bigger one.
- **Taking the money and promising the date.** The vetting provider sets the date.
- **Pushing back in public.** It forces the CEO to defend her position.
- **Leaving the vetting contract unsigned.** Every day it waits moves week 17.

## 10. Follow-ups an interviewer might ask

**The CEO says no and insists on "all shippers". What now?**
Disagree and commit, in writing: put the $1.53M-a-month exposure in a short note, ask that compliance and the CFO see it, and then deliver the safest version of her call, such as autonomy with mandatory broker vetting on non-trusted carriers. Escalating around her is a last resort for a safety or legal issue, not for a schedule.

**The vetting provider says onboarding will take 14 weeks, not 10. What changes?**
All-shipper autonomy moves to week 21, after the renewal. Tell the CEO that week, and offer the anchors more trusted-carrier coverage instead.

**An anchor asks on stage whether the agent books with any carrier. What do you say?**
"With carriers that have hauled for us for two years with no claims, and we check every change to their payment details." It's true and it's a selling point.

**The shadow gate fails at week 6. What goes on stage?**
The co-pilot for everyone, with the anchors' autonomy dated for week 12 after a second shadow run. Tell the CEO and the anchors before the summit, not after.

**How would you have avoided this?**
Share a one-page capability calendar with the CEO each quarter, showing what can be announced and when. Leaders announce what they can see.
